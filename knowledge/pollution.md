# Pollution and Biodiversity

## What is Pollution?

Pollution occurs when harmful substances or forms of energy enter the environment at levels that can cause adverse effects.

Examples include:
- Chemical pollution
- Pesticide contamination
- Nutrient pollution
- Plastic pollution
- Air pollution
- Water pollution
- Soil contamination

## Why is Pollution Important?

Pollution can affect:
- Soil organisms
- Plants
- Insects
- Birds
- Aquatic organisms
- Water quality
- Soil health
- Ecosystem functions

The effects depend on the pollutant, concentration, exposure duration, environmental conditions, and organisms affected.

## Pollution and Biodiversity

Pollution can create environmental stress and may reduce the abundance or distribution of sensitive species.

For example, excessive nutrients entering water bodies can contribute to eutrophication, which can alter aquatic ecosystems and affect biodiversity.

Some pesticides can also affect non-target organisms in addition to the organisms they are intended to control.

## Pollution and Soil Health

Soil contamination can affect soil organisms and biological processes.

Excessive or inappropriate chemical inputs may also affect soil and water quality.

Therefore, pollution should be considered alongside:
- Soil organic carbon
- Soil pH
- Soil moisture
- Land use
- Biodiversity indicators

## Pollution and Water Availability

Pollutants can reduce the quality of available water even when the physical amount of water remains unchanged.

This means biodiversity assessments should distinguish between:
- Water quantity
- Water quality

Both can influence species survival.

## Possible Interventions

Depending on the pollutant and local conditions:

- Identify and monitor pollution sources
- Reduce unnecessary chemical inputs
- Follow appropriate pesticide application practices
- Maintain vegetation buffer zones near water bodies
- Prevent contaminated runoff
- Protect soil from erosion
- Monitor soil and water quality

Interventions should be selected based on the specific pollutant and its measured concentration.

## Important Caution

Pollution is not a single environmental variable.

A recommendation should identify the pollutant and exposure conditions where possible rather than assuming that all pollution has the same ecological effect.

## Metrics to Monitor

- Pollutant concentration
- Soil contamination
- Water quality
- Nutrient levels
- Pesticide residues
- Species richness
- Species abundance
- Indicator species
