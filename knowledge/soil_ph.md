# Soil pH and Biodiversity

## What is Soil pH?

Soil pH measures how acidic or alkaline a soil is.

The pH scale generally ranges from 0 to 14:
- Below 7 = acidic
- 7 = neutral
- Above 7 = alkaline

Soil pH strongly influences nutrient availability, microbial activity, and which plants can grow successfully.

## Why is Soil pH Important?

Different plants and soil organisms have different pH requirements.

Soil pH can affect:
- Nutrient availability
- Plant growth
- Soil microbial activity
- Decomposition
- Species composition
- Soil biological diversity

Extremely acidic or alkaline conditions can restrict the availability of some nutrients and make conditions unsuitable for certain organisms.

## Soil pH and Biodiversity

Soil pH can influence which plant and microbial species are able to survive in an area.

Changes in pH can therefore change species composition and soil biological communities.

However, the relationship is context-dependent. Soil type, climate, vegetation, and land management also influence biodiversity.

## Soil pH and Land Use

Agricultural practices can influence soil chemical properties.

Long-term intensive management may alter soil conditions, while appropriate soil management can help maintain suitable conditions for plants and soil organisms.

## Possible Interventions

Depending on soil testing and crop requirements, management may include:

- Testing soil pH regularly
- Selecting crops suited to the existing soil pH
- Applying appropriate soil amendments when scientifically justified
- Maintaining organic matter
- Avoiding unnecessary chemical inputs

Soil amendments should not be applied simply to reach a universal pH value. The appropriate target depends on the crop, soil type, and local conditions.

## Important Caution

Soil pH alone does not determine biodiversity.

A change in pH may benefit some species while disadvantaging others. Recommendations should consider soil type, vegetation, climate, and land-use conditions together.

## Metrics to Monitor

- Soil pH
- Crop/plant growth
- Plant species richness
- Soil microbial diversity
- Nutrient availability
