# Soil Organic Carbon (SOC)

## What is SOC?

Soil Organic Carbon (SOC) is the carbon stored in soil from plant and animal material.

It is an important indicator of soil health.

## Why is SOC important?

Higher soil organic carbon can support:

- Better soil structure
- Better water-holding capacity
- Nutrient cycling
- Soil biological activity
- Protection against soil degradation

Low SOC can indicate degraded or less resilient soil conditions.

## Environmental relationships

SOC is connected with multiple environmental factors:

### SOC ↔ Soil Moisture
Soils with more organic matter can generally retain more water. This can help reduce water stress, although the effect depends on soil type, climate and management.

### SOC ↔ Biodiversity
Healthy soils can support diverse communities of microorganisms and soil organisms.

### SOC ↔ Land Management
Practices such as maintaining soil cover, returning organic residues and using diversified cropping systems can influence SOC.

## Possible interventions

Depending on local conditions, possible management approaches include:

- Cover crops
- Returning crop residues
- Crop rotation
- Reduced soil disturbance
- Diversified cropping

## Important caution

SOC responses vary depending on climate, soil type, crop system, management practice and time period.

The system should not claim that an intervention will produce a fixed SOC increase without supporting evidence.
