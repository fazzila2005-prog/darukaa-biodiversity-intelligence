# Rainfall

## What is Rainfall?

Rainfall is the amount of precipitation received by an area over a given period.

It is an important part of the water cycle and strongly affects soil and ecosystems.

## Why is Rainfall important?

Rainfall influences:

- Soil moisture
- Plant growth
- Water availability
- Crop productivity
- Habitat conditions
- Species survival

## Environmental relationships

### Rainfall ↔ Soil Moisture

Rainfall replenishes water in the soil.

Periods of low rainfall can reduce soil moisture and create water stress.

### Rainfall ↔ Biodiversity

Changes in water availability can affect vegetation and the resources available to species.

Species that depend on particular moisture conditions may be affected when rainfall patterns change.

### Rainfall ↔ Temperature

High temperatures can increase water demand and evaporation.

Therefore, low rainfall combined with high temperature can create stronger water stress than either factor alone.

## Possible interventions

Depending on local conditions, possible approaches include:

- Maintaining vegetation and soil cover
- Improving soil water retention
- Protecting water sources
- Using appropriate drought-resilient management
- Increasing habitat diversity where suitable

## Important caution

Rainfall effects vary according to:

- Location
- Season
- Soil type
- Temperature
- Vegetation
- Existing water availability

The system should consider multiple environmental variables instead of using rainfall alone.
