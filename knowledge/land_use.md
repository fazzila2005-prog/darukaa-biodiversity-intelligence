# Land Use and Biodiversity

## What is Land Use?

Land use describes how humans use and manage land.

Examples include:
- Agriculture
- Forest
- Grassland
- Urban areas
- Plantations
- Pasture
- Wetlands

Land use affects habitat availability, soil conditions, water availability, and the species that can survive in an area.

## Why is Land Use Important?

Different land-use systems provide different amounts and types of habitat.

Land-use changes can affect:
- Habitat availability
- Habitat diversity
- Species richness
- Soil health
- Water availability
- Carbon storage
- Ecosystem functions

## Land Use and Habitat Diversity

A landscape containing different habitat types can provide different resources and shelter for organisms.

For example, a landscape containing crops, grass strips, trees, ponds, and native vegetation may provide more types of habitat than a landscape dominated by a single land-use type.

## Land Use and Species Richness

Changes from natural or diverse habitats to simplified land-use systems can reduce the variety of habitats available to species.

However, the effect depends on the type of land use, management practices, landscape structure, and the species being considered.

## Agriculture and Biodiversity

Agricultural landscapes can support biodiversity when management maintains or creates habitat diversity.

Possible approaches include:

- Crop rotation
- Crop diversification
- Intercropping
- Maintaining native vegetation
- Planting field margins
- Maintaining hedgerows
- Agroforestry
- Protecting nearby natural habitats

These practices can influence multiple environmental variables simultaneously, including habitat diversity, soil organic carbon, soil moisture, and species richness.

## Land Use and Habitat Fragmentation

When continuous natural habitat is divided into smaller isolated patches, organisms may have fewer opportunities to move between suitable habitats.

Maintaining habitat connections such as vegetation corridors can help maintain landscape connectivity for some species.

## Possible Interventions

Depending on local conditions:

- Increase habitat diversity
- Maintain native vegetation
- Reduce unnecessary habitat removal
- Create vegetation buffers or field margins
- Connect isolated habitat patches where appropriate
- Diversify agricultural systems

## Important Caution

There is no single land-use strategy that improves every biodiversity indicator in every location.

Recommendations should consider:
- Local species
- Existing habitats
- Climate
- Soil conditions
- Water availability
- Landscape structure
- Human land-use requirements

## Metrics to Monitor

- Land-use / land-cover proportions
- Habitat diversity
- Habitat connectivity
- Species richness
- Vegetation cover
- Soil organic carbon
- Soil moisture
