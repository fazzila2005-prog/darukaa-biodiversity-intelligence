# Soil Moisture

## What is Soil Moisture?

Soil moisture is the amount of water stored in the soil.

It is important for plants, soil organisms and ecosystem functioning.

## Why is Soil Moisture important?

Adequate soil moisture can support:

- Plant growth
- Root development
- Soil organisms
- Nutrient cycling
- Survival of vegetation

Very low soil moisture can create water stress for plants and organisms.

## Environmental relationships

### Soil Moisture ↔ Rainfall

Rainfall is an important source of soil water.

Low rainfall over an extended period can reduce soil moisture and increase water stress.

### Soil Moisture ↔ SOC

Soil organic carbon and organic matter can influence the ability of soil to retain water.

However, the effect depends on soil type, climate and management.

### Soil Moisture ↔ Biodiversity

Water availability affects vegetation and therefore can influence habitat conditions and the ability of species to survive.

## Possible interventions

Depending on local conditions, possible approaches include:

- Maintaining soil cover
- Using appropriate cover crops
- Reducing unnecessary soil disturbance
- Improving soil organic matter
- Using water-efficient management practices

## Important caution

The relationship between soil moisture and biodiversity depends on:

- Climate
- Soil type
- Vegetation
- Season
- Water availability
- Land management

The system should avoid making universal claims.
