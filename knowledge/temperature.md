# Temperature and Biodiversity

## What is Temperature?

Temperature describes how hot or cold an environment is.

Temperature is an important climate variable because organisms have limits to the environmental conditions in which they can survive, grow, and reproduce.

## Why is Temperature Important?

Temperature can influence:

- Plant growth
- Animal activity
- Species survival
- Reproduction
- Water loss
- Soil biological activity
- Ecosystem processes

## Temperature and Biodiversity

Different species have different temperature ranges in which they can survive.

Changes in temperature can therefore alter:
- Species distributions
- Species abundance
- Timing of biological events
- Community composition
- Ecosystem processes

Species that cannot adapt, move, or otherwise respond to changing temperature conditions may experience increased stress.

## Temperature and Water Availability

Temperature and water availability can interact.

Higher temperatures can increase evaporative demand and water loss from soil and vegetation. When rainfall is also low, plants and organisms may experience greater water stress.

Therefore, temperature should not always be evaluated independently from rainfall and soil moisture.

## Temperature and Soil

Temperature can influence biological activity in soils, including processes involving microorganisms and decomposition.

The effect depends on soil moisture, organic matter, vegetation, and other environmental conditions.

## Possible Interventions

Depending on the ecosystem and land-use system:

- Maintain vegetation cover
- Protect existing trees and native vegetation
- Increase habitat diversity
- Maintain soil cover
- Improve soil organic matter where appropriate
- Protect water sources
- Use locally appropriate plant species

Vegetation can influence local microclimatic conditions, but the magnitude of these effects depends on the landscape and climate.

## Important Caution

Temperature alone cannot determine whether biodiversity will increase or decrease.

Recommendations should consider temperature together with:
- Rainfall
- Soil moisture
- Habitat
- Land use
- Species requirements
- Local climate conditions

## Metrics to Monitor

- Mean temperature
- Minimum and maximum temperature
- Temperature extremes
- Soil moisture
- Vegetation cover
- Species richness
- Species abundance

## Evidence Source

Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) — Climate Change and Land and biodiversity-related assessment reports.
